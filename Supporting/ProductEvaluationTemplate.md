## Purpose and Instructions

Throughout the semester, you will maintain one Product Evaluation document for each of your classmates. These documents are intended to help you listen actively, evaluate how each product develops, and prepare constructive comments and questions for class discussion.

Before the first Product Pitch session:

1. Create one copy of this template for every student in the class except yourself.
2. Save each copy in a directory named `ProductEvaluation` within your private class repository.
3. Name each file using the presenter's name. For example:

   `ProductEval-Mike-Aguilar.md`

Do not place your own name in the document. The instructor can identify you through your private repository.

Your evaluations are part of your class-participation record and are graded for completion. To receive completion credit:

- Maintain a separate document for each classmate.
- Complete the required fields for every presentation you attend.
- Enter your observations during the presentation session rather than reconstructing them later.
- Commit and push your updated documents after class. You do not need to commit after each individual presentation.

Short phrases and one- or two-sentence responses are appropriate. Spelling, grammar, and whether your assessment agrees with the presenter are not graded. If you are absent, no evaluation or makeup entry is required unless the instructor directs otherwise.

Write candidly, professionally, and constructively. Portions of your feedback may be incorporated into an anonymous summary provided to the presenter at selected project milestones. Your name will not appear in that summary; however, because this is a small class, complete anonymity cannot be guaranteed.

Whenever possible, voice constructive comments and questions during the class discussion. Discussion time may not allow every student to speak after every presentation, but you should be prepared to contribute.

# Product Pitch

Each student will present an initial Product Pitch describing the proposed customer, customer problem, solution, and product. As an audience member, you are evaluating the pitch from the perspective of a potential stakeholder or investor.

Take brief notes while the student presents. The complete evaluation should require approximately five minutes in total, including the time spent taking notes during the presentation. Limit each response to one or two sentences.

## Customer and Problem

Who is the target customer, and what problem does the product seek to solve?

[Response]

## Proposed Value

How would the proposed solution help the target customer? Why might the customer prefer it to the current approach?

[Response]

## Most Promising Aspect

What appears most promising about the proposed product?

[Response]

## Most Important Question or Concern

What is the most important question or concern about the product's usefulness, financial or analytical foundation, or feasibility? Frame this constructively as a question you could raise during the discussion.

[Response]

## Recommendation

What is one concrete recommendation that could strengthen the proposed product?

[Response]

# Status Updates

Each student will provide several brief Status Updates during the semester. These presentations describe progress in areas such as data acquisition, analytics, interface development, testing, and deployment.

Take notes while the student presents. After the presentation, you will have only a short period to finalize your entry. The complete evaluation should require approximately two to three minutes in total, including the time spent taking notes during the presentation. A short phrase or one sentence per prompt is sufficient.

For every Status Update you attend, copy the section below and replace `YYYY-MM-DD` with the presentation date.

## Status Update - YYYY-MM-DD

**Progress:** What is the most important new evidence of progress demonstrated during this update?

[Response]

**Next Step:** What single action or deliverable should the data scientist prioritize before the next update?

[Response]

**Question for Discussion:** What aspect of the update would you like the presenter to explain more fully? Write one specific question about the presenter's evidence, reasoning, trade-offs, or an unresolved challenge.

[Response]
